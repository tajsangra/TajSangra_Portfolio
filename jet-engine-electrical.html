Project Overview

Why a jet engine?

Supporting a fully custom engine build.

A group of my peers took on the challenge of designing and building a low-cost jet engine entirely from scratch. Their experience was focused more on the mechanical side than electronics, so they asked me to design and build the electrical and control system for the engine.

System goals

Control the key systems needed for the first engine tests.

The first iteration needed variable-speed control of the fuel pump, spark-plug ignition, RPM sensing, and an LCD for live operating information. I designed the electronics so these functions could be controlled from one enclosure and expanded as testing progressed.

Electrical System

Arduino Mega

Main system controller

The Arduino Mega was chosen for its large I/O count, allowing the pump, solenoid, ignition, RPM sensor, and display to be controlled from one low-cost board with room for expansion.

Solenoid

Fuel-flow shutoff control

The normally closed solenoid blocks fuel flow when unpowered. A 4-channel relay lets the Arduino switch the solenoid’s higher-power supply using a low-power control signal.

RPM Sensor

Reflective rotational-speed sensing

A reflective IR sensor reads a half-silver, half-black rotating target, producing one pulse per revolution for RPM calculation. I validated the method on a Tormach Tech Pro router bit at just under 10,000 RPM.

Spark Plug & Ignition

High-voltage ignition system

A ZVS driver switches DC power at high frequency into a step-up transformer, producing the high voltage needed to create a spark across the plug gap.

Fuel Pump

Variable-speed fuel delivery

The pump is powered from a separate supply through a motor driver. The Arduino sends a low-power control signal to vary pump speed without carrying the pump current itself.

LCD Display

Live operating information

The LCD shows pump speed, pump on/off status, and live RPM so the main operating values stay visible during testing.

Bench Testing

Future Improvements

Add Temperature Sensing

Add temperature sensing at a critical hot section of the engine so thermal conditions can be monitored during testing and displayed alongside RPM and pump information.

Add Safety Systems & Failsafes

Add a physical emergency stop and software failsafes that can shut down the pump and ignition if unsafe conditions occur, such as an invalid sensor reading, excessive temperature, or loss of expected RPM feedback.
