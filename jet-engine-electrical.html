01 / OVERVIEW

Project Overview

Why a jet engine?

Supporting a fully custom engine build.

A group of my peers took on the challenge of designing and building a low-cost jet engine entirely from scratch. Their experience was focused more on the mechanical side than electronics, so they asked me to design and build the electrical and control system for the engine.

System goals

Control the key systems needed for the first engine tests.

The first iteration needed variable-speed control of the fuel pump, spark-plug ignition, RPM sensing, and an LCD for live operating information. I designed the electronics so these functions could be controlled from one enclosure and expanded as testing progressed.

02 / SYSTEM

Electrical System

Each major subsystem is documented separately so the controller, switching, motor drive, ignition, sensing, display, and power hardware can be explained clearly.

01

Arduino Mega

Main system controller

The Arduino Mega was chosen for its large number of available I/O pins, which gives the system enough headroom to control several subsystems from a single controller while still leaving room for expansion. It also provides a low-cost and well-supported platform for developing and testing the first version of the control system.

02

Solenoid

Fuel-flow shutoff control

The fuel solenoid is normally closed, so fuel flow is blocked when it is not energized. Because the Arduino cannot supply the voltage or current required by the solenoid directly, a 4-channel relay module lets the Arduino use a low-power control signal to switch the solenoid's separate power-supply circuit on and off.

03

Fuel Pump

Variable-speed fuel delivery

The fuel pump is powered from a separate power supply through a motor driver. The Arduino sends the driver a low-power control signal, allowing it to vary the pump speed without having to source the pump's current itself. This separates the high-current motor load from the microcontroller while still giving the software direct control over fuel-pump output.

04

Spark Plug & Ignition

High-voltage ignition system

The spark plug is driven by a ZVS high-frequency driver and a step-up transformer. The ZVS circuit takes DC power from the supply and rapidly switches current through the transformer primary, producing a high-frequency alternating waveform. The transformer then steps this voltage up to the high level needed to create a spark across the spark-plug gap.

05

RPM Sensor

Reflective rotational-speed sensing

The planned RPM measurement uses a reflective infrared sensor aimed at a rotating bolt with one half left reflective silver and the other half painted black. The change in reflected light produces one repeatable reflectance cycle per revolution, which the Arduino can count to calculate RPM. I tested the sensing approach by colouring half of a Tormach Tech Pro router bit black and running it at just under 10,000 RPM.

06

LCD Display

Live operating information

The LCD currently displays the commanded pump speed, whether the pump is enabled or off, and the live RPM reading from the sensor. This keeps the key operating information visible from the control enclosure during bench testing.

Bench Testing

Individual subsystems were tested separately before full integration so ignition and fluid-system behavior could be checked independently.

04 / FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Future Improvements

Areas to refine in the next revision of the electrical and control system.

Cleaner wiring and connectors

Replace temporary bench wiring with a more structured harness and dedicated connectors so the system is easier to assemble, troubleshoot, and service.

More integrated packaging

Bring more of the external electrical hardware into a cleaner enclosure layout while keeping ignition and engine-side connections accessible during testing.

Expanded monitoring and safety

Add more operating feedback and protection as the engine moves from subsystem testing toward full integrated runs.
